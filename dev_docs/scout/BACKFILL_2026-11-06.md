# Scout backfill, 2025-11-06 to 2026-11-06

A one-off test of the idea scout: collect a year of Hacker News, score every title for fit with
Field, and check whether the scores would have caught the things Field actually took from HN. The
full ranked list (1,171 titles scoring 3 to 5) is
[`backfill_2026-11-06_ranked.tsv`](backfill_2026-11-06_ranked.tsv).

## What was run

| Step | Details |
| --- | --- |
| Collect | HN Algolia `search_by_date`, one query per day, stories with 50 or more points: **24,152 stories**, no failed or capped days |
| Score | Eight cheap-model agents (Haiku) scored every title against a written rubric (below) without seeing the list of past catches. Each scored about 3,000 titles. |
| Candidates | 823 scored 3, 323 scored 4, 25 scored 5, so **348 a year score 4 or more (about 7 a week)** |
| Check | A one-word repo search for each top candidate, then two web-fetch agents read the ten best pages for figures, license and source |

Rubric, in short: Field reimplements long algorithm explainers with figures and bridges new
creative-coding, music, graphics, media or visualization languages and runtimes. Interaction
ideas, papers with code and datasets also count. Be strict: 1 to 3% of titles should get 4 or 5.

## Where Field's ideas came from

For about forty topics, I took the first commit and searched HN (at any point level) for the 90
days before it.

**Clear HN origins (9):** Sonic Pi v5 (3 days before the first commit, 430 points), hyperbezier
curves (5 days, 202), TALA Is Open-Source (same day, 316), the Gaussian splat strawberry (same day,
530), Drawgent on Excalidraw (1 day, 178), Easel, an IDE built in Clojure (13 days, 183), MorphoHDL
→ Morpho (12 days, 99), the WFC hex map (53 days, 580) and the tetrahedral-cage ray tracing paper
(47 days, 123). Pi's ideas came from a ChatGPT analysis of the repo, not a news post.

**No HN trace:** Lenia, Physarum, Braitenberg, matrix profile, Faust, ORCA, Loris, Manifold,
Ceres, Gaussian processes and stigmergy never appeared as HN stories. Strudel, Gray-Scott and flow
matching did appear, but at 2 to 5 points. "A Decade of Slug" ran in March with 760 points, but
`slug_paths.md` only arrived six months later.

So HN accounts for a minority of what Field has absorbed. The rest comes from long practice. A
scout widens the input to that judgment; it doesn't replace it.

## Did the scorers find the past catches?

| Past catch | Points | Score |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sonic Pi v5 | 430 | 4 |
| Hyperbezier curves | 202 | 4 |
| Gaussian splat strawberry / splat videogame | 530 / 236 | 4 / 4 |
| Drawgent | 178 | 4 |
| WFC hex map | 580 | 4 |
| Tetrahedral cages | 123 | 4 |
| TALA Is Open-Source | 316 | 3 |
| Easel | 183 | 3 |
| MorphoHDL | 99 | 3 |

- **Score of 3 or more: 9 of 9 topics**, at about 22 a week. **Score of 4 or more: 6 of 9**, at
  about 7 a week. Nothing past scored 5.
- The three misses at 4 are the same kind of item: a small, odd tool or language whose title
  doesn't say why it matters (a diagram layout engine, one person's IDE, "growing circuits"). Titles
  can't distinguish these, so the stage that reads the page has to.
- **A keyword prefilter loses recall.** An earlier version filtered titles by keywords first. It
  dropped 3 of 12 seed stories (Sonic Pi v5, TALA, an ONNX demo) because their titles contain no
  domain words. Score everything with a model instead: about 70 titles a day.
- **A points threshold hides exactly what Marc's lineage cares about.** Strudel, Gray-Scott and flow
  matching posts had 2 to 5 points. Topic queries against Field's existing subsystems need a
  threshold near zero.
- Batch 5's agent reported reading 3,000 of its 3,019 lines. The final rows of every batch scored
  low anyway, so the gap is probably harmless, but the routine should check coverage
  mechanically, not take the agent's word for it.

## Cards: the best misses

The pages below were read by web-fetch agents. "Figures" are the acceptance tests: each one is
reproduced from real code, or the job isn't done.

### New ideas

**Eigendrum: draw a shape, hear it as a drum** (243 + 216 points, Aug 2026).
[page](https://eigendrum.com/) · FEM eigenmodes of the drawn region (−∇²u = λu, Dirichlet
boundary, generalized eigenproblem Kφ = λMφ); a strike projects the mallet position onto the modes,
with Rayleigh damping.
*What Field adds:* the drum is drawn on the drawing layer, and `notation` already reads strokes.
Modes go straight into the audio engine, and the shape can be animated, so the timbre changes as it
morphs. Isospectral drums (Kac I/II) make a natural figure.
*Figures:* the 16-mode list (each mode played alone), mesh view, the Kac isospectral pair, polar
equation input. *License:* "source available, non-commercial". Reimplement from the method, which
is textbook, and copy no code.
*Nearby in Field:* `GpuPressureProjectionProbe`, the drawing layer and notation; no eigenmode solver
yet.

**Neural cellular automata: Growing NCA (Distill) plus Cells2Pixels (SIGGRAPH 2026)**
(133 + 208 points). [Distill](https://distill.pub/2020/growing-ca/) ·
[Cells2Pixels](https://cells2pixels.github.io/) · 16-channel state, Sobel perception, an
~8K-parameter per-cell MLP, stochastic updates, pool training with damage. Cells2Pixels adds an
implicit decoder that renders a coarse grid at any resolution, and on meshes.
*What Field adds:* it sits between the alife work (Lenia, Physarum, stigmergy) and the neural work
(neural particles, flow matching). Train in Python, run the update rule as a compute shader, and
paint damage with the brush live.
*Figures:* the playground's grow / persist / regenerate modes, the damage-regeneration experiment,
the rotated perception experiment, and the MeshNCA texture on a mesh.
*License:* Distill text CC-BY; Cells2Pixels site CC-BY-SA, code license unstated.

**Atmospheric scattering: "Rendering the Sky, Sunsets, and Planets"** (537 points, May 2026).
[page](https://blog.maximeheckel.com/posts/on-rendering-the-sky-sunsets-and-planets/) · Raymarched
Rayleigh, Mie and ozone terms, a nested light-march for optical depth, a planet shell, eclipses;
cites Hillaire's lookup-table approach.
*What Field adds:* a GPU lesson for the renderer, and sky light for the path tracer and splat
scenes. *Figures:* the raymarch-steps widget, the pitch/altitude sky widget, the Mie/ozone sun-angle
widget, the eclipse sequence. *License:* unstated, so build from the method.

**Ciechanowski's explainers: Sound, Cameras and Lenses, Lights and Shadows, Airfoil, Moon**
(273 / 549 / 241 / 531 / 267 points; five of his older essays reposted this year).
[Sound](https://ciechanow.ski/sound/) · [Cameras and Lenses](https://ciechanow.ski/cameras-and-lenses/)
· They carry no code, only physics. Each one is a list of 10 to 20 interactive figures, which makes
it an acceptance-test list for a reimplementation.
*What Field adds:* "Sound" becomes a live page where the drawn-waveform synthesizer, the Fourier
decomposer and the string/membrane modes all play through Field's audio engine. "Cameras and
Lenses" is the thin-lens model the path tracer's camera could adopt: aperture, focus distance,
cos⁴ vignetting, Bayer demosaicing.
*License:* all rights reserved, so write it from the physics and reuse none of his figures. Name
him in the page.

**Pattern Collider: quasiperiodic tilings via de Bruijn multigrids** (74 points).
[repo](https://github.com/aatishb/patterncollider) · Penrose, Ammann–Beenker and Socolar tilings,
where clicking a grid line highlights its ribbon. MIT.
*What Field adds:* tilings as FLines, which can then be pen-plotted, extruded through the mesh
booleans or used as a GPU instancing domain. Small, a single night.

**Shape-aware ASCII rendering** (1,353 points, the year's top-scoring 4).
[page](https://alexharri.com/blog/ascii-rendering) · Six sampling circles per cell give a shape
vector, matched to glyphs by nearest neighbour, with contrast sharpening; GPU pipeline.
*What Field adds:* an output-pass effect for any renderer, and it touches `madp_chargrid`.
*License:* unstated, so build from the method.

### Touching work already in Field

These are the second use for the scout: news that adds to a subsystem Field already has.

- **Dogalog, a Prolog live-coding music environment** (82 points, MIT,
  [repo](https://github.com/danja/dogalog)) next to **Datalog in Field**: rules such as
  `event(kick, 60, 100, T) :- euc(T, 4, 16, 4, 0)` decide when notes play. Field's Datalog plus its
  pattern engine could do this with Field's own scheduler, without a bridge.
- **Cagire, a Forth step-sequencer** (111 points, **AGPL-3.0+, so compatible**,
  [site](https://cagire.raphaelforment.fr)) next to **Forth in Field**: each step holds a
  program, with Ableton Link and two-way OSC. Bridge it over OSC, or take the each-step-is-a-program
  design for Field's Forth.
- **"Let your AI agents paint big arrows, boxes and text on your screen"** (421 points) next to
  **`notation` and Drawgent**: evidence that agents drawing on the canvas, which Field already does,
  is broadly wanted. A docs or outreach item more than a build item.
- **Un-0, images from coupled oscillators** (191 points) next to the Kuramoto material in
  `TEMPORAL_MEMORY_EXPERIMENTS.md`.
- **Particle Lenia** (54 points, [page](https://znah.net/lenia/)) next to Lenia. Its 54 points
  would sit below many thresholds, which is the point made above.
- **Sparse-strips CPU 2D rendering** (281 points; Raph Levien's lineage, like hyperbezier) next
  to FLine rendering.

## What this changes about the routine

1. **Score every title with a model; never prefilter by keyword.** About 70 titles a day costs
   nothing.
2. **Read the page; never judge from the headline.** The title pass only throws out what is
   plainly off-domain (politics, business, AI-industry news, hardware launches). Every other link
   is fetched and read, and the card is written from the page. That's about 30 to 40 pages a day.
   A headline doesn't tell a human reader whether a link is a fit (Marc, 2026-11-06), so it
   shouldn't decide for a model either.
3. **Two kinds of card:** *new* (a reimplementation or bridge) and *touches existing work* (names
   the `dev_docs/` page or subsystem it adds to). The second kind needs topic queries with no
   points threshold, generated from Field's own subsystem list (Lenia, Faust, Forth, Datalog,
   splats, ...).
4. **Search the repo before a card is written.** Slug, Kalman and Forth were already in Field. A
   one-line search caught them, but the card should name the nearest existing page.
5. **Check license on every card**, separating "copy code" from "build from the method". This year:
   Cagire is AGPL and Pattern Collider and Dogalog are MIT, but Eigendrum is non-commercial and
   Ciechanowski and Heckel are all rights reserved.
6. **Reposts are a signal.** Five older Ciechanowski essays were reposted this year, and old
   explainers keep scoring. The ledger should track repeat appearances.
7. **Record where each idea came from** on the page it lands as. Until today the repo recorded
   none.

Other sources to add, given how many past catches had no HN trace: Graphics Programming Weekly,
SIGGRAPH paper lists, lobste.rs, llllllll.co, and topic queries against Field's own subsystems.
