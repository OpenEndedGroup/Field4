## The counterfactual, and why the brief's version of it is false

The brief asked for "a mask with no structure condenses to itself". Measured, **0 of 400** random
16-step masks do, and that is not the search misbehaving. For a boolean mask to be un-shortened by
run-length replication it must have no two adjacent equal cells; over two symbols that forces strict
alternation, which is `[x ~]*8`. A sparse mask really does contain runs, and naming a run is a true
statement — every one of those 400 re-renders exactly.

So two sharper claims are asserted in its place. Over the 400, every whole-span figure the search
named was recomputed from `bjorklund` / `kolakoskiMask` and compared to the mask: 6 named, 0 wrong.
And noise compresses to 0.613 where a real figure reaches 0.127, a separation with a floor on it. The
"comes back as itself" property is then asserted where it *is* reachable — over five atoms, where 200
of 200 sequences with no repeat at any divisor come back byte-identical.

## What was not done

- **RectSL.** `condense` was not wired into the musical layer. It reads as a time-domain operation on
  a bar and RectSL's rhythm verbs lay figures out in space; I could not find a shape for it that was
  not decoration. Left undone deliberately rather than half-done.
- **The three remaining limits** (nested alternation, polymeter, the Kolakoski alphabet) are pinned as
  tests with a note on what each would cost. A fourth came *off* the list tonight — a rhythm figure
  over a repeated *term* rather than a repeated atom, so `[bd cp](3,8)` now condenses to itself
  instead of to 26 characters.
- **Optimality.** The result is the shortest string *found* under a beam of 4, not a proof that none
  is shorter. `PatCondensation.bounded` says when a bound was hit; on everything measured it is false.

## Risks and unknowns

- **The oracle is the whole safety argument.** If `patCycleSignature` ever stops distinguishing two
  audibly different event sets, a wrong condensation would pass. It compares part, whole, value and
  annotations per cycle and deliberately ignores source spans. Annotations beyond `:n` are the place
  I would look first if this ever went wrong; the current behaviour is to *refuse* patterns carrying
  them, which is the safe direction.
- **Period detection is a window, not a proof.** A target is checked over `[0,P)`, `[P,2P)` and
  `[2P,3P)`. A pattern with a period longer than 8 cycles, or one that changes at cycle 30, would be
  condensed on the strength of what was looked at. Randomised targets are refused precisely because
  they fail this check, and two comparison windows are used rather than one for that reason.
- **`showPatCondense` and `markdown` both write channel `main`.** That is how `midi_clip` does it and
  the demo was observed working, but it is a convention rather than a guarantee.
- **Cost.** A 16-slot mask condenses in ~1 ms and `kolakoskiMask(64)` expands 69 spans. A 256-slot
  cycle with many divisors is the untested corner; the node budget is the backstop and it reports
  when it fires.

## Demo

```bash
./field.sh dev_docs/agents/demos/pattern_condense --read-only
```

Three boxes. **Box 1 — Condense a rhythm:** poke `rhythms`; anything you add comes back as a named
figure or spelled out, decided by character count alone. **Box 2 — Invent nothing:** poke `seed` or
`density`; the *wrong* count must stay 0 whatever you set, and it is recomputed from
`bjorklund`/`kolakoskiMask` rather than believed off the label. **Box 3 — Close the loop:** poke
`bar`; `60 ~ ~ 60 60 ~ 60 ~` comes back as `60(k8)`, `60 60 ~ 60 ~ ~ 60 ~` as `60(g4) 60(1,4,2)`.
