When the door closed behind me and I saw my eyes were going to be useless in the dark, I took myself in hand.

Then let go, hands lifting—feeling around in front of me.

This as my feet crept forward, fingers brushing against whatever my fears birthed in the black.

—such phantasms wriggling right back into their own nonexistence.

A smile widened my lips, faintly surprising me as I felt it there.

Nothing like the shock of the rhinoceros that stormed through my head next, panting through my nose…

As I stepped forward, a pebble scraped under my shoe. Soon I’d be wanting to take shoes and socks off—naked feet to match naked hands.

And my clothes?—Those too.

Belt undone, pants dropping to my ankles, I stumble around, hopping on one foot, with arms flailing—till one arm suddenly hugs tight to a smooth stone column it finds right there.

Marble. Going back to antiquity.

Is that a tiny ripple of water sounding over there? The aqueduct I instantly erect above my head collapses on my considering it.

Later, plodding along insensibly, one slow foot in front of the other, the ground was starting to crumble under my weight, toes digging in for purchase.

Each toe could smell the earth it touched.

It was my skin that seemed to know of the rain about to fall, drenching me.

Plunging through buckets of downpour, clanging my head against their metal rims.

A whole school of ideas swam before me, but I could hook only one: don’t believe what you see.

Yet every so often, out in the periphery, stray motes would light up under thoughts I had no idea were mine.

They weren’t. They were pollinating a land of vague promise.

My eyes accelerated their useless darting around in the dark. Likewise, my ears strained harder to pinpoint any sounds in the muffled silence.

My nostrils opened up wide, inhaling the cool clear air in hope of finding fragrance.

Faintly hinted, the heady scent of a rose.

Hungering for color, my mind instantly dyed itself a sultry red.

Soon as I caught sight of what I was thinking, though, I drooped.

Still, my longing for the land I’d already half-promised myself hadn’t withered away. The rain might come again, this time after the seeds had fallen.

My feet already sensed what was coming instead. Their naked soles flinched from the sharp jabs of the now rock-strewn ground.

And yet, as the terrain began to climb and the rocks gave way to boulders, my spirit reached for whatever promise it could find—recalling how in the hard folds of a rock-face a sapling sometimes finds a precarious perch in the silt collected there.

My muscles burned—nowhere near the top, I was down to bone.

My words found a few last handholds of their own once my body collapsed.

How hard it is for a butterfly flitting around in the air to pronounce itself… until it alights on a flower—
the way this stray idea alights on the end of its stem: sipping.

And now this sentence, pulled taut, goes nowhere: though somewhere a vestigial muscle did its work.
